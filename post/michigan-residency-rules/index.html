Bottom line: Check the exceptions first

There is a structural barrier here, not just a high bar. For a typical dependent undergraduate the standard route is effectively closed, so the exceptions further down are the realistic path. The U-M Guidelines list as NOT demonstrating Michigan residence, among other things, that 'you have continuous physical presence in Michigan for one year or more,' 'you possess a Michigan driver's license or voter's registration,' and 'you have paid Michigan income tax or filed Michigan resident income tax returns' - the entire standard residency playbook is pre-emptively disclaimed. On top of that, anyone 24 or younger who is primarily involved in educational pursuits or not financially self-supporting is presumed dependent, and 'if you are a dependent student, and both your parents are legal residents of another state, you are presumed to be a nonresident.' The controlling rule is University policy (no statute) - University of Michigan Board of Regents, Guidelines for Qualifying for In-State Tuition, administered by the Residency Classification Office.

Who can qualify

If you are independent

'You are presumed to be a dependent of your parents if you are 24 years of age or younger and (1) have been primarily involved in educational pursuits, or (2) have not been financially self-supporting through employment.' A dependent student whose parents are both legal residents of another state is presumed to be a nonresident, and must both prove Michigan is their permanent legal residence and demonstrate they have severed all out-of-state ties.

If your parents claim you

A dependent student qualifies through a parent's or spouse's Michigan permanent domicile; where parents are divorced, having at least one parent or parent-in-law who is a permanent Michigan legal resident can support qualification, but the student must still show Michigan is their permanent legal residence and that out-of-state ties are severed.

How to establish residency

  1. Establish a permanent home in Michigan. Keep one fixed address, and keep proof of it for the whole period.
  2. Show intent to stay. These are the acts this state looks for:
    • where you live, work and attend school, and where you have previously lived, worked and attended school
    • where your parents or guardians live
    • permanent (non-student, non-internship) Michigan employment
    • Michigan driver's license (listed as insufficient on its own)
    • Michigan voter registration (listed as insufficient on its own)
    • Michigan resident income tax returns (listed as insufficient on its own)
    • property ownership or Michigan property taxes (listed as insufficient on its own)
    • severing all out-of-state ties
  3. Document your finances. Be ready to prove how you support yourself and that nobody out of state claims you as a dependent.
  4. Cut ties to your old state. Surrender the old license, cancel the old voter registration, and move your vehicle registration.
  5. Petition your university. File the reclassification application with the registrar or residency office before their deadline.

Documents to collect

  • Application for Resident Classification (completed truthfully and timely)
  • evidence of permanent, non-student Michigan employment
  • lease or property records
  • tax returns (student's and parents')
  • documentation severing out-of-state ties
  • Michigan middle and high school transcripts (Attendance Track)
  • DD-214 or active-duty orders (Service Track)

Deadlines

Applications for Resident Classification must be received by 5:00 p.m. on September 30 (Fall), January 31 (Winter), and July 31 (Spring/Summer). Late applications are assessed a nonrefundable $300 late fee and are accepted only through the last published day of classes of that term.

Shortcuts and exceptions

These groups may qualify faster, or right away:

  • Attendance Track: attended an accredited Michigan middle school and high school, graduated from a Michigan high school or earned a Michigan GED, and matriculated to U-M within 40 months (per the current Registrar guidance)
  • Service Track: active duty U.S. military, honorably discharged veterans, Public Health Service officers, and their spouses and dependent children
  • qualification through a spouse's Michigan permanent domicile

The detail most people miss

U-M's guidelines are deliberately decoupled from every other definition of residency: they state that the guidelines 'are independent of other state rules or regulations governing residency for other purposes, including income and property tax liability or eligibility to vote or drive.' So a student can be a Michigan resident for taxes, voting and driving and still be billed out-of-state. The one reliable door is the Attendance Track, which does NOT require residency at all - it turns purely on having attended Michigan middle and high school and matriculating within the published window.

Common mistakes

  • Starting late. The clock runs before enrollment, so a plan that begins in your first semester is already behind.
  • Keeping old-state ties. An out-of-state license, voter registration, or car tag can sink an otherwise strong case.
  • Thin documentation. Reviewers decide on paper. If you cannot prove it, it did not happen.
  • Assuming your school reads the rule loosely. Campuses vary in how strictly they apply the same statute.
