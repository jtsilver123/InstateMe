How to Get In-State Tuition in Washington

InstateMe research·Jul 10, 2026

Difficulty

Flagship school

Bottom line: Worth pursuing with planning

Winnable, but you will need to prove financial independence and keep tight documentation for the whole qualifying period. WAC 250-18-030 presumes any nonresident enrolled in more than six credits is present primarily for education and bars that enrollment period from counting toward the 12 months. WAC 250-18-035 then requires financial independence across two full calendar years, counts tuition itself as a living expense, and excludes gifts, cash earnings and parent PLUS loans from the income side. The controlling rule is RCW 28B.15.012 and RCW 28B.15.013; WAC 250-18-030 and WAC 250-18-035.

Who can qualify

If you are independent

The student must not have been claimed as a tax dependent for the calendar year before applying AND must not be claimed for the calendar year of application, and must not have received significant financial assistance in any form across both those years. The student must show that personal income met living expenses, including tuition, for both calendar years. Financial aid grants, scholarships and loans in the student's own name count as income, but personal loans, parent PLUS loans, gifts and cash earnings are expressly excluded. UW's administrative gloss is 51% of living expenses. The burden is on the student.

If your parents claim you

A dependent student qualifies if one or both parents or legal guardians have maintained bona fide Washington domicile for at least one year before the term begins (RCW 28B.15.012(2)(b)).

How to establish residency

  1. Live in Washington for 12 continuous months. Keep one fixed address, and keep proof of it for the whole period.
  2. Show intent to stay. These are the acts this state looks for:
    • Washington vehicle or personal property registration
    • Washington driver's license or state ID card
    • continuous full-time employment in Washington
    • address on federal and prior-state income tax returns
    • Washington voter registration
    • primary residence via title, lease or rent receipts
    • residency status at schools attended outside Washington
    • Washington checking/savings accounts and safety deposit boxes
    • selective service registration address
    • membership in professional, business or civic organizations
    • receipt of public assistance
    • state claimed for public office or judicial actions
    • state of hunting/fishing licenses
    • state where a custodial parent has a child in public schools
  3. Document your finances. Be ready to prove how you support yourself and that nobody out of state claims you as a dependent.
  4. Cut ties to your old state. Surrender the old license, cancel the old voter registration, and move your vehicle registration.
  5. Petition your university. File the reclassification application with the registrar or residency office before their deadline.

Documents to collect

  • prior-year federal tax return or written explanation of non-filing
  • W-2s or most recent pay stub from each employer
  • prior state's income tax return
  • for trust/529/UTMA accounts: establishment date, balance at high school graduation and full transaction ledger
  • first page of a parent's tax return showing dependents if age 25 or under
  • Washington driver's license, permit or ID card
  • Washington vehicle registration
  • Washington voter registration with date
  • lease, UW housing statement or notarized statement from head of household
  • 12 months of physical presence evidence such as bank or credit card statements showing in-person transactions

Deadlines

The 30th calendar day following the first day of instruction of the quarter, which is statutory under RCW 28B.15.013(4). Reclassification takes effect the quarter the evidence was filed and is not retroactive.

Shortcuts and exceptions

These groups may qualify faster, or right away:

  • active duty stationed in Washington and Washington National Guard members (RCW 28B.15.012(2)(g))
  • servicemembers who entered as Washington residents and are stationed out of state (2)(h)
  • spouses, state registered domestic partners and dependents of the above (2)(j)
  • veterans eligible for VA educational assistance (2)(k)
  • veterans with 10+ years of service entering within 3 years (2)(l) and their spouses and children under 26 (2)(m)
  • 38 U.S.C. § 3679(c)(2) covered individuals (2)(o)
  • HB 1079 affidavit pathway for undocumented Washington high school graduates (2)(e))
  • American Indian students who are members of tribes whose traditional boundaries included Washington (RCW 28B.15.0131)
  • graduate service appointees and university employees at 20+ hours/week, and certain refugees (WAC 250-18-060)

The detail most people miss

The 12-month clock does not start when you arrive, it starts on the date your LAST legal tie is in place. UW states that 'all domicile must be established prior to the start of this consecutive 12-month period' and directs new arrivals to get a Washington driver's license or state ID within 30 days of arrival. Someone who moves in August but waits until January for the license has reset the clock a full year, roughly a $31,900 swing. A related trap: WAC 250-18-035(5) makes a 529 or trust account opened before the student entered high school a rebuttable presumption of financial DEPENDENCE.

Common mistakes

  • Starting late. The clock runs before enrollment, so a plan that begins in your first semester is already behind.
  • Keeping old-state ties. An out-of-state license, voter registration, or car tag can sink an otherwise strong case.
  • Thin documentation. Reviewers decide on paper. If you cannot prove it, it did not happen.
  • Assuming your school reads the rule loosely. Campuses vary in how strictly they apply the same statute.
