How to Get In-State Tuition in North Dakota

InstateMe research·Jul 10, 2026

Difficulty
Easy
Time required
12 monthsN.D.C.C. 15-10-19.1(2)(b) makes an individual age 18 or over a resident student for tuition purposes if the person is a legal resident of North Dakota and has resided in the state after reaching age 18 for the twelve months immediately prior to the beginning of the academic term. Nothing in the statute suspends or tolls that twelve month clock while the person is enrolled, and there is no credit hour cap. UND asks for a North Dakota driver's license or state ID issued at least 10 months before the application date plus continuous proof of North Dakota address for the full 12 months, with no gap of more than two weeks between leases.

Bottom line: Worth pursuing

A clear domicile pathway with no bar on students. The residency clock keeps running while you are enrolled, so this is winnable if you plan ahead and document carefully. North Dakota is one of the cleanest domicile pathways in the country. The statute sets a flat 12 month legal residence test for anyone 18 or older, imposes no financial independence test, contains no presumption that a student is present only for education, has no credit hour cap, and does not stop the clock while the applicant is enrolled full time. The general residence statute, N.D.C.C. 54-01-26, likewise contains no student exclusion. On top of that the statute lists many independent qualifying categories, including graduating from a North Dakota high school, being a benefited NDUS employee, and having been a legal resident for any three consecutive years within the prior six years. Documentation is the main burden, not eligibility. The controlling rule is N.D.C.C. 15-10-19.1 (nonresident and resident student for tuition purposes defined); general residence rules at N.D.C.C. 54-01-26; NDUS Procedure 504.

Who can qualify

If you are independent

Not required. Neither N.D.C.C. 15-10-19.1 nor the NDUS campus residency applications impose a financial self-sufficiency test or ask the applicant to prove that parents stopped supporting them. Age 18 plus 12 months of legal residence is the independent-student test.

If your parents claim you

Yes. Under 15-10-19.1(2)(a) a student is a resident if a guardian, custodial parent, or parents are legal residents of North Dakota and have resided in the state for twelve months, or if the student is a dependent child whose custodial parent moved into the state within the last twelve months with intent to establish legal residency for a period of years. Verification is the parent's North Dakota driver's license or ID plus the first page of the parent's most recent federal IRS tax transcript.

How to establish residency

  1. Live in North Dakota for 12 continuous months. Keep one fixed address, and keep proof of it for the whole period.
  2. Show intent to stay. These are the acts this state looks for:
    • Obtain a North Dakota driver's license or state identification card, ideally at least 10 to 12 months before applying
    • Register a motor vehicle in North Dakota
    • Maintain a continuous North Dakota lease, mortgage, or rental history with no gap over two weeks
    • File a North Dakota resident state income tax return
    • Register to vote in North Dakota
  3. Document your finances. Be ready to prove how you support yourself and that nobody out of state claims you as a dependent.
  4. Cut ties to your old state. Surrender the old license, cancel the old voter registration, and move your vehicle registration.
  5. Petition your university. File the reclassification application with the registrar or residency office before their deadline.

Documents to collect

  • North Dakota driver's license or state ID card, dated at least 10 to 12 months before application
  • Twelve consecutive months of North Dakota leases, mortgage or home purchase documents, or utility and rent payment records
  • North Dakota motor vehicle registration
  • Prior year North Dakota resident income tax return dated at least 12 months before the term
  • For the dependent route, the parent or guardian's North Dakota license plus the first page of their federal IRS tax transcript
  • For military and veteran routes, current military orders, separation orders, or VA paperwork

Deadlines

UND requires the residency application to be submitted by the first day of the semester, and once approved the student does not reapply while continuously enrolled. NDSU publishes hard per-term deadlines, for example September 11 for fall 2026 and January 29 for spring 2027. Rate adjustments are made only for the current term and no prior terms are refunded.

Shortcuts and exceptions

These groups may qualify faster, or right away:

  • Graduated from a North Dakota public, private, or home high school
  • Full-time active duty armed forces member, National Guard member, armed forces reserve member, or veteran as defined in N.D.C.C. 37-01-40
  • Spouse or dependent of any of the above, including transferees of Post-9/11 GI Bill entitlement
  • Benefited employee of the North Dakota University System, and that employee's spouse or dependent
  • Spouse of any other person who is a resident for tuition purposes
  • Legal resident of North Dakota for at least three consecutive years within the six years before the academic term
  • Child, spouse, widow, or widower of a veteran killed in action, totally disabled from service-connected causes, a prisoner of war, or missing in action
  • Covered individual under Section 702 of Public Law 113-146 (38 U.S.C. 3679)
  • Returned Peace Corps volunteer certified by a Peace Corps director
  • Minnesota residents pay a separate reciprocity rate rather than the full nonresident rate

The detail most people miss

The three-in-six lookback in 15-10-19.1(2)(h) restores resident tuition to anyone who was a legal North Dakota resident for any three consecutive years within the previous six years, no matter where they have lived since. The statute also expressly names returned Peace Corps volunteers as residents, which is unusual.

Common mistakes

  • Starting late. The clock runs before enrollment, so a plan that begins in your first semester is already behind.
  • Keeping old-state ties. An out-of-state license, voter registration, or car tag can sink an otherwise strong case.
  • Thin documentation. Reviewers decide on paper. If you cannot prove it, it did not happen.
  • Assuming your school reads the rule loosely. Campuses vary in how strictly they apply the same statute.
