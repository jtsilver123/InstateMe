How to Get In-State Tuition in Massachusetts

Bottom line: Only with a strong case

There is a strong presumption against students who moved here for school. You can still win, but you need clear evidence and usually financial independence. The Board of Higher Education policy requires the student (or the parent of an unemancipated student) to have resided in Massachusetts 'for purposes other than attending an educational institution (including a private educational institution) for twelve months,' and states flatly that 'residency is not acquired by mere physical presence in Massachusetts while the person is enrolled in an institution of higher education.' The burden of proof rests entirely on the student. The controlling rule is Massachusetts Board of Higher Education, Residency Status for Tuition Classification Purposes (adopted Apr. 19, 2007, eff. July 1, 2007).

Who can qualify

If you are independent

An 'emancipated person' is one who has attained age 18 and is financially independent of his or her parents (or, if under 18, whose parents have surrendered care, custody and earnings, or who is legally married, or who has no parent). Proof of emancipation requires items such as a statement from the student and parents certifying no financial support, certified copies of both the student's and the parents' federal and state tax returns, and copies of federal financial aid applications. 'Reliance on Massachusetts sources for financial support' is a listed indicium of residence.

If your parents claim you

An unemancipated student qualifies if the parent (or the custodial parent where parents are divorced, separated or unmarried, or the court-appointed legal guardian) has resided in Massachusetts for purposes other than attending an educational institution for the 12 months immediately preceding the student's entry or reentry.

How to establish residency

  1. Live in Massachusetts for 12 continuous months. Keep one fixed address, and keep proof of it for the whole period.
  2. Show intent to stay. These are the acts this state looks for:
    • driver's license
    • car registration
    • voter registration
    • state/federal tax returns
    • employment pay stub (permanent employment in a position not normally filled by a student)
    • signed lease or rent receipt
    • utility bills
    • Massachusetts high school diploma
    • military home of record
    • record of parents' residency for an unemancipated person
  3. Document your finances. Be ready to prove how you support yourself and that nobody out of state claims you as a dependent.
  4. Cut ties to your old state. Surrender the old license, cancel the old voter registration, and move your vehicle registration.
  5. Petition your university. File the reclassification application with the registrar or residency office before their deadline.

Documents to collect

  • at least 2 documents from the state's list, dated within one year of the start of the semester
  • certified copies of federal and state income tax returns (student's and parents')
  • signed lease or rent receipt
  • employment pay stubs
  • driver's license and car registration
  • voter registration
  • utility bills
  • statement from student/parents certifying no financial support (for emancipation)

Deadlines

The statewide policy sets no fixed deadline: a student 'may at any time request the institution to reclassify him or her,' and each institution 'may adopt supplementary rules governing any procedures, deadlines.' At UMass Amherst, reclassification applications are handled by the Office of the Bursar.

Shortcuts and exceptions

These groups may qualify faster, or right away:

  • New England Board of Higher Education Regional Student Program (NEBHE Tuition Break)
  • active-duty Armed Forces members stationed in Massachusetts, plus spouse and dependent children
  • spouse of a person classified or eligible to be classified as a Massachusetts resident
  • lawful immigrants, permanent residents, refugees and asylees meeting the same requirements as citizens
  • students funded by the Massachusetts Rehabilitation Commission or other Commonwealth public assistance programs
  • full-time faculty, professional and classified staff of the public higher education system, plus spouses and dependent students
  • continued classification: anyone registered as a Massachusetts resident stays eligible during continuous attendance until the degree is attained

The detail most people miss

Two traps. First, reclassification is only prospective: 'Any change in a student's classification as the result of a request for reclassification or an appeal will be retroactive only to the beginning of the semester during which the institution makes the final decision' - so a slow decision costs you the earlier semesters outright. Second, one of the enumerated indicia of residence is 'continuous physical presence in Massachusetts during periods when not an enrolled student,' meaning summers and breaks spent back in the old home state actively count against you.

Common mistakes

  • Starting late. The clock runs before enrollment, so a plan that begins in your first semester is already behind.
  • Keeping old-state ties. An out-of-state license, voter registration, or car tag can sink an otherwise strong case.
  • Thin documentation. Reviewers decide on paper. If you cannot prove it, it did not happen.
  • Assuming your school reads the rule loosely. Campuses vary in how strictly they apply the same statute.
