Bottom line: Only with a strong case

There is a strong presumption against students who moved here for school. You can still win, but you need clear evidence and usually financial independence. Va. Code § 23.1-502 requires proof by clear and convincing evidence and bars institutions from giving ANY weight to evidence that has not existed for a full year, while § 23.1-503(G) presumes continuously enrolled non-Virginia students are present for educational purposes and § 23.1-503(H) makes reclassification prospective only. Students under 24 are presumed dependent unless they fit one of seven narrow exceptions. The controlling rule is Va. Code § 23.1-502 (with §§ 23.1-500, 23.1-501, 23.1-503, 23.1-509, 23.1-510).

Who can qualify

If you are independent

An 'independent student' is one whose parents have surrendered the right to his care, custody and earnings, do not claim him as a tax dependent, and have ceased providing substantial financial support (§ 23.1-500). A student under 24 is presumed dependent unless a veteran/active duty, a graduate or professional student, married, a ward of the court, parentless, supporting legal dependents, or able to show financial self-sufficiency by clear and convincing evidence (§ 23.1-501). There is NO codified >50% self-support percentage; the SCHEV Guidelines instead require that resources originate from the student's own personal funds, and expressly exclude gifts, trust funds, family loans, parent PLUS loans and prepaid tuition plans regardless of when they were established.

If your parents claim you

A dependent student is rebuttably presumed to hold the domicile of the parent or legal guardian who claims him as a tax exemption or provides substantial financial support, so it is the PARENT who must satisfy the full one-year clear-and-convincing test; where parents are separated, the student may claim through the parent he lives with, the one claiming him for taxes, or the one providing substantial support.

How to establish residency

  1. Live in Virginia for 12 continuous months. Keep one fixed address, and keep proof of it for the whole period.
  2. Show intent to stay. These are the acts this state looks for:
    • continuous residence for at least one year
    • state to which income taxes are filed or paid
    • driver's license
    • motor vehicle registration
    • voter registration
    • employment
    • property ownership
    • sources of financial support
    • military records
    • a written offer and acceptance of employment following graduation
    • any other social or economic relationships within and outside the Commonwealth
  3. Document your finances. Be ready to prove how you support yourself and that nobody out of state claims you as a dependent.
  4. Cut ties to your old state. Surrender the old license, cancel the old voter registration, and move your vehicle registration.
  5. Petition your university. File the reclassification application with the registrar or residency office before their deadline.

Documents to collect

  • Application for Virginia In-State Educational Privileges (claiming through a parent, spouse or guardian)
  • Application for Virginia Student Status (claiming on own domicile)
  • Financial Statement Form estimating income and expenses for the year prior to the term
  • federal and state tax returns for the prior year, on request
  • active-duty orders or proof of honorable discharge for military claims
  • proof of two years of Virginia high school attendance plus graduation and two years of Virginia resident tax returns for tuition-equity claims

Deadlines

Applications must be submitted before the first day of classes of the term; UVA states applications received after that date will not be reviewed, and recommends filing at least 60 days ahead. No retroactive reclassification.

Shortcuts and exceptions

These groups may qualify faster, or right away:

  • active duty, retired military, surviving spouses and veterans electing Virginia, plus dependents, one-year requirement waived (§ 23.1-504)
  • dependents of active duty or activated reservists assigned to Virginia, D.C. or a contiguous state (§ 23.1-505)
  • veterans residing in Virginia, in-state regardless of domicile (§ 23.1-506(A)(4))
  • nonresidents employed full time in Virginia for a year and paying Virginia income tax, plus their tax-dependent children (§ 23.1-506(A)(1)-(2))
  • children of servicemembers who filed Virginia returns 10+ years during service (§ 23.1-506(A)(8))
  • foreign service officers and dependents (§ 23.1-506(A)(7))
  • refugees and Special Immigrant Visa holders (§ 23.1-506(A)(9))
  • victims of human trafficking (§ 23.1-506(A)(10))
  • tuition equity: 2 years at a Virginia high school plus graduation and 2 years of Virginia tax returns, regardless of immigration status (§ 23.1-505.1)
  • Academic Common Market (§ 23.1-506(B)(1))
  • community college 30-mile reciprocity (§ 23.1-506(C))

The detail most people miss

In-state status is not permanent, Va. Code § 23.1-509 imposes a surcharge equal to roughly the full unsubsidized cost of education once a Virginia student passes the 'credit hour threshold' of 125% of the credits needed for the degree (150 credits on a 120-credit program). Double majors and major switches are the usual triggers. AP/IB credits earned in high school, dual-enrollment credits earned before the diploma, remedial courses and non-applicable transfer credits are excluded from the count.

Common mistakes

  • Starting late. The clock runs before enrollment, so a plan that begins in your first semester is already behind.
  • Keeping old-state ties. An out-of-state license, voter registration, or car tag can sink an otherwise strong case.
  • Thin documentation. Reviewers decide on paper. If you cannot prove it, it did not happen.
  • Assuming your school reads the rule loosely. Campuses vary in how strictly they apply the same statute.
