Bottom line: Only with a strong case

There is a strong presumption against students who moved here for school. You can still win, but you need clear evidence and usually financial independence. State rules presume anyone who comes from another state and enrolls in a New Jersey school is here primarily for education and has not established domicile, and staying in New Jersey over vacations does not by itself rebut that. A dependent undergraduate's domicile is fixed by the parents' domicile, and independent status turns on criteria most 18 to 22 year olds cannot meet by self-support alone. The controlling rule is N.J.A.C. 9A:5-1.1 and following.

Who can qualify

If you are independent

New Jersey does not use a dollar self-support test you can satisfy by paying your own bills. You count as independent only if you are 24 or older, a veteran, an orphan or former ward of the court, married, a graduate student, or supporting legal dependents. Everyone else is dependent and judged on the parents' domicile.

If your parents claim you

A dependent undergraduate takes the domicile of their parents, so the realistic route is for the parents to establish genuine New Jersey domicile at least 12 months before the term and document it with a state-certified New Jersey resident tax return.

How to establish residency

  1. Live in New Jersey for 12 continuous months. Keep one fixed address, and keep proof of it for the whole period.
  2. Show intent to stay. These are the acts this state looks for:
    • File a New Jersey resident income tax return for the year before enrollment
    • Own or hold a long-term lease on a permanent New Jersey residence
    • Obtain a New Jersey driver's license or state ID
    • Register a vehicle in New Jersey
    • Register to vote in New Jersey
    • Execute a sworn, notarized statement of New Jersey domicile
    • Maintain New Jersey bank accounts, utility accounts, and employment
  3. Document your finances. Be ready to prove how you support yourself and that nobody out of state claims you as a dependent.
  4. Cut ties to your old state. Surrender the old license, cancel the old voter registration, and move your vehicle registration.
  5. Petition your university. File the reclassification application with the registrar or residency office before their deadline.

Documents to collect

  • Notarized Rutgers Residency Analysis Form
  • State-certified copy of the New Jersey resident income tax return for the prior year
  • Notarized affidavit of New Jersey domicile
  • Current and prior-year federal tax returns
  • Property deed, long-term lease, or landlord letter
  • New Jersey driver's license, vehicle registration, and voter registration
  • Utility or phone bills at a New Jersey address

Deadlines

Rutgers accepts residency change requests no later than 60 days after the term starts, supporting evidence within 30 days of the form, and appeals within 30 days of notification.

Shortcuts and exceptions

These groups may qualify faster, or right away:

  • New Jersey Tuition Equality Act: in-state tuition for students who attended a New Jersey high school three or more years and graduated there, regardless of immigration status
  • Military personnel and their dependents living in New Jersey
  • Recipients of Montgomery or Post-9/11 GI Bill benefits
  • Permanent residents, refugees, and asylees approved before the term begins

The detail most people miss

New Jersey's primary evidence bar is documentary, not declarative. Rutgers requires a notarized affidavit plus a state-certified copy of the New Jersey resident tax return obtained from the Division of Taxation, and a self-printed photocopy of your filed return is not enough. Your driver's license, car registration, and voter registration count only as supplementary evidence, so a student who did all the usual intent steps but has no certified state tax return has not met the standard.

Common mistakes

  • Starting late. The clock runs before enrollment, so a plan that begins in your first semester is already behind.
  • Keeping old-state ties. An out-of-state license, voter registration, or car tag can sink an otherwise strong case.
  • Thin documentation. Reviewers decide on paper. If you cannot prove it, it did not happen.
  • Assuming your school reads the rule loosely. Campuses vary in how strictly they apply the same statute.
