How to Get In-State Tuition in Kansas

InstateMe research·Jul 10, 2026

Difficulty
Medium

12 monthsa continuous 365-day period immediately prior to the first day of classes
Flagship school

Bottom line: Worth pursuing with planning

Winnable, but you will need to prove financial independence and keep tight documentation for the whole qualifying period. K.A.R. 88-3-2 provides that 'if a person is continuously enrolled for a full academic program... it shall be presumed that the student is in Kansas for educational purposes, and the burden shall be on the student to prove otherwise,' and no factor counts unless it has existed for at least a year before enrollment. KU further requires that the student have supported themself for 12 months paying their own tuition, fees and expenses, support from out-of-state parents, trust funds, bank loans or nonresident scholarships defeats the claim. The controlling rule is K.S.A. 76-729; K.A.R. 88-3-1 through 88-3-13.

Who can qualify

If you are independent

KU requires that the student has 'supported yourself for at least twelve months, paying your own tuition, fees and expenses if enrolled in school during this period.' Financial support from out-of-state parents in the previous 365 days that helped the student stay in school results in nonresident classification, as does money from other out-of-state sources such as trust funds, bank loans or nonresident scholarships.

If your parents claim you

A person under 18 takes the residence of the custodial parent or guardian; under K.A.R. 88-3-13, a dependent student with divorced parents qualifies if one parent maintains Kansas resident status, and the privilege continues with continuous fall/spring enrollment.

How to establish residency

  1. Live in Kansas for 12 continuous months. Keep one fixed address, and keep proof of it for the whole period.
  2. Show intent to stay. These are the acts this state looks for:
    • continuous presence in Kansas, except brief temporary absences, during periods when not enrolled as a student
    • employment in Kansas (beyond positions typically held by students)
    • payment of Kansas state resident income taxes
    • reliance on Kansas sources for financial support
    • acceptance of an offer of permanent employment in Kansas
    • admission to a licensed profession in Kansas
    • ownership of a home in Kansas
    • commitment to an educational program indicating intent to remain permanently in Kansas
  3. Document your finances. Be ready to prove how you support yourself and that nobody out of state claims you as a dependent.
  4. Cut ties to your old state. Surrender the old license, cancel the old voter registration, and move your vehicle registration.
  5. Petition your university. File the reclassification application with the registrar or residency office before their deadline.

Documents to collect

  • Kansas resident income tax returns
  • employment verification and pay records showing Kansas-source, non-student employment
  • Kansas driver's license
  • proof of Kansas home ownership
  • evidence of self-support including payment of own tuition and living expenses
  • documentation of severance of ties to other states
  • official Kansas high school transcript (for the high school graduate category)

Deadlines

Petitions must be submitted by the 30th calendar day after the first day of classes in the term for which residency is sought; applications may be submitted as early as 75 days in advance.

Shortcuts and exceptions

These groups may qualify faster, or right away:

  • current armed forces members, Post-9/11-eligible veterans, and their spouses and dependents (K.A.R. 88-3-8a)
  • veterans previously stationed in Kansas or with pre-service Kansas residency
  • Kansas high school graduates enrolling within six months of graduation who were Kansas residents at graduation or within the preceding 12 months (K.A.R. 88-3-10)
  • employees of Kansas Regents institutions at 0.4 FTE or more, plus spouses and dependent children of full-time employees (K.A.R. 88-3-9)
  • recruited or transferred employees establishing Kansas domicile for employment, and their spouses and dependents, for up to 12 months (K.A.R. 88-3-11)
  • prior or current Haskell Indian Nations University attendance
  • specified tribal enrollments
  • undocumented students with 3+ years of Kansas high school attendance plus a citizenship-application affidavit

The detail most people miss

Kansas expressly refuses to give independent weight to the very things students assume will prove residency. Under K.A.R. 88-3-2, none of the following alone is sufficient evidence: voting or voter registration in Kansas, employment in positions normally held by students, a Kansas lease, statements of intent to be a Kansas resident, a spouse's Kansas residence, Kansas vehicle registration, obtaining a Kansas driver's license, paying Kansas personal property tax, or continuous enrollment at a Kansas institution.

Common mistakes

  • Starting late. The clock runs before enrollment, so a plan that begins in your first semester is already behind.
  • Keeping old-state ties. An out-of-state license, voter registration, or car tag can sink an otherwise strong case.
  • Thin documentation. Reviewers decide on paper. If you cannot prove it, it did not happen.
  • Assuming your school reads the rule loosely. Campuses vary in how strictly they apply the same statute.
