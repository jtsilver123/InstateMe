Bottom line: Only with a strong case

There is a strong presumption against students who moved here for school. You can still win, but you need clear evidence and usually financial independence. State law presumes a person attending school full time has not established Connecticut domicile unless domicile is clearly shown independent of enrollment, plus a further presumption that where parents provide financial help, the student's domicile follows the parent. UConn also requires a notarized application backed by 12 months of license, lease, vehicle registration, voter registration, and utility records. The controlling rule is Conn. Gen. Stat. Sections 10a-28 through 10a-30.

Who can qualify

If you are independent

Required for the emancipated pathway. State law defines an emancipated person as someone 18 or older whose parents have entirely surrendered the right to their care, custody, and earnings and have no legal obligation to support them. UConn requires W-2 or 1099 evidence of independence plus the front page of both your and your parents' signed state and federal returns.

If your parents claim you

An unemancipated student takes the parent's domicile, so you are in-state if that parent is Connecticut-domiciled. UConn requires the parent's license, lease or mortgage, voter and vehicle registration, utility bills, and returns claiming you as a dependent, generally dated at least six months before the semester.

How to establish residency

  1. Live in Connecticut for 12 continuous months. Keep one fixed address, and keep proof of it for the whole period.
  2. Show intent to stay. These are the acts this state looks for:
    • Connecticut driver's license, permit, or state ID held for 12 months
    • Connecticut vehicle registration and car insurance for 12 months
    • Connecticut voter registration for 12 months
    • A Connecticut resident income tax return filed from a Connecticut address
    • A signed Connecticut lease or mortgage held 12 months
    • Utility bills in your name for 12 months
    • Connecticut employment income shown by a W-2 or 1099 with a Connecticut address
  3. Document your finances. Be ready to prove how you support yourself and that nobody out of state claims you as a dependent.
  4. Cut ties to your old state. Surrender the old license, cancel the old voter registration, and move your vehicle registration.
  5. Petition your university. File the reclassification application with the registrar or residency office before their deadline.

Documents to collect

  • Notarized UConn Application for In-State Tuition
  • Connecticut driver's license, permit, or state ID
  • Signed Connecticut lease or mortgage
  • Twelve months of utility bills in your name
  • Connecticut vehicle registration and car insurance
  • Connecticut voter registration
  • Signed state and federal tax returns for both you and your parents
  • W-2 or 1099 showing financial independence and a Connecticut address

Deadlines

Applications are due July 1 for fall and December 1 for spring. Review takes four to six weeks and the application must be notarized.

Shortcuts and exceptions

These groups may qualify faster, or right away:

  • Connecticut high school graduates with two years of attendance plus graduation, including undocumented students
  • Active-duty military stationed in Connecticut, plus spouses and dependents
  • Military spouses and dependents keep in-state status if the service member transfers after matriculation
  • Veterans with 90 or more days of active service who live in Connecticut, and GI Bill users
  • The spouse of a person classified as an in-state student
  • Students whose parents move out of Connecticut after matriculation keep in-state status through graduation
  • Certain dependent visa holders, pending asylum and permanent residency applicants, and DACA recipients

The detail most people miss

You may not need to change residency at all at one UConn campus. Since fall 2024, all incoming first-year and transfer students with New England residency who enroll at UConn Avery Point receive the regional Tuition Break rate of 175 percent of in-state for their entire undergraduate enrollment, with no requirement that their major be unavailable in their home state. At every other UConn campus, the Tuition Break is limited to specific approved majors.

Common mistakes

  • Starting late. The clock runs before enrollment, so a plan that begins in your first semester is already behind.
  • Keeping old-state ties. An out-of-state license, voter registration, or car tag can sink an otherwise strong case.
  • Thin documentation. Reviewers decide on paper. If you cannot prove it, it did not happen.
  • Assuming your school reads the rule loosely. Campuses vary in how strictly they apply the same statute.
