How to Get In-State Tuition in Indiana

Bottom line: Only with a strong case

There is a strong presumption against students who moved here for school. You can still win, but you need clear evidence and usually financial independence. IU requires proof that permanent residence in Indiana was established 'for a predominant purpose other than to establish residency in the State of Indiana for in-state tuition and fees,' and students under 24 enrolled as undergraduates are treated as dependents whose status follows their parents. Indiana is less absolute than its reputation, however: IU states that enrollment during the 12-month period does not by itself defeat a claim. The controlling rule is Ind. Code § 21-14-2-1; Indiana University Policy VPSO-07 (Rules Determining Resident and Nonresident Student Status for IU Fee Purposes).

Who can qualify

If you are independent

A student under 24 enrolled in an undergraduate program is a dependent student, 'financially reliant on a parent(s) or legal guardian(s) and who can be claimed as a dependent by a parent(s) or legal guardian(s) on federal taxes.' Independent status attaches at 24, or to graduate/professional students, married students, students with dependents, orphans, wards, emancipated persons, unaccompanied youth and former foster youth. Under-24 students claiming emancipation must supply notarized parental statements on the level of support provided and the date parents last claimed them as a dependent.

If your parents claim you

A dependent student is a resident if the permanent residence of the parents or legal guardians was established in Indiana for a predominant purpose other than obtaining in-state tuition at least 12 consecutive months before the first day of the semester, and the 12-month requirement may be waived if the parents moved to Indiana more recently for a qualifying purpose.

How to establish residency

  1. Live in Indiana for 12 continuous months. Keep one fixed address, and keep proof of it for the whole period.
  2. Show intent to stay. These are the acts this state looks for:
    • establishment of a permanent residence in Indiana, 'the location the individual lives the majority of the time and is the location the individual remains when not called elsewhere for labor, studies, or other special or temporary purposes'
    • Indiana state income tax returns (military families must supply the most recent three consecutive years)
    • at least two forms of documentation plus a written statement and attestation, per IU's standardized residency application
  3. Document your finances. Be ready to prove how you support yourself and that nobody out of state claims you as a dependent.
  4. Cut ties to your old state. Surrender the old license, cancel the old voter registration, and move your vehicle registration.
  5. Petition your university. File the reclassification application with the registrar or residency office before their deadline.

Documents to collect

  • IU Application for Classification as a Resident Student with written statement and attestation
  • at least two forms of documentation establishing permanent residence in Indiana
  • Indiana state income tax returns (three consecutive years for military families)
  • notarized statement from parents on support provided and last year claimed as a tax dependent (emancipation claims under 24)
  • notarized statement from parents on their new Indiana residence and employment (parents relocating)
  • court documents establishing legal guardianship
  • permanent resident card, where applicable

Deadlines

Students have until the last day of the effective semester to deliver a completed reclassification application to the Office of the Registrar; applications may be submitted as early as two months before the start of the term.

Shortcuts and exceptions

These groups may qualify faster, or right away:

  • veterans, military members and GI Bill recipients (IU Policy VPSO-08)
  • reciprocal-county agreements with qualifying Ohio and Kentucky counties (IU campuses other than Bloomington)
  • Indiana Partner discounted rate for residents of qualifying states (IU campuses other than Bloomington)
  • students experiencing homelessness with no fixed residence

The detail most people miss

Contrary to Indiana's strict reputation, IU states that being enrolled does not automatically disqualify you: 'If a person can provide convincing evidence that the move to Indiana was without the predominant purpose of attending an institution of higher education, future resident student classification should not be affected by University enrollment during the 12-month residency period, even if such enrollment is on a full-time basis.' Also, the 12-month clock can be waived outright when a student's parents relocate to Indiana for a qualifying non-tuition purpose.

Common mistakes

  • Starting late. The clock runs before enrollment, so a plan that begins in your first semester is already behind.
  • Keeping old-state ties. An out-of-state license, voter registration, or car tag can sink an otherwise strong case.
  • Thin documentation. Reviewers decide on paper. If you cannot prove it, it did not happen.
  • Assuming your school reads the rule loosely. Campuses vary in how strictly they apply the same statute.
