How to Get In-State Tuition in Texas

Bottom line: Worth pursuing

We run our full residency program in this state. Texas is one of the harder states for a traditional college age student, and the reason is written directly into the rule. 19 Texas Administrative Code 21.24 presumes that an individual whose initial purpose for moving to Texas is to attend an institution of higher education as a full time student does not have the required intent to make Texas a domicile, and that presumption can only be overcome by clear and convincing evidence. On top of that, an independent student cannot establish domicile through generic ties like a lease and a driver license alone. The person must fit one of three economic bases: gainful employment, sole or joint marital ownership of residential real property, or ownership and customary management of a Texas business. Gainful employment is defined narrowly as income covering at least one half of tuition, fees, and living expenses or an average of at least twenty hours per week, and employment conditioned on student status such as work study, stipends, fellowships, and research or teaching assistantships is explicitly excluded. This is realistically achievable for someone who genuinely relocates and works for twelve months before enrolling. It is very hard for a student who enrolls first and tries to convert later. The controlling rule is Texas Education Code Sections 54.052 through 54.064; 19 Texas Administrative Code Chapter 21, Subchapter B, Sections 21.22, 21.24, and 21.25.

Who can qualify

If you are independent

Texas does not impose a separate income threshold test. Instead the question is dependent versus independent status. Under 19 Texas Administrative Code 21.22 a dependent is a person under 18 who is not emancipated, or a person who qualifies as a dependent for federal income tax purposes. Texas Education Code 54.052(b) presumes the domicile of a dependent's parent is the domicile of the dependent. An independent individual must satisfy one of the three domicile bases in his or her own name, and the gainful employment base itself functions as a de facto self support test because the income must cover at least half of tuition, fees, and living expenses or represent an average of at least twenty hours of work per week.

If your parents claim you

A dependent student qualifies through a parent. The parent must have established domicile in Texas not later than one year before the census date of the term and maintained that domicile continuously for the year preceding the census date, satisfying one of the same three bases. The dependent takes the parent's domicile by presumption under Texas Education Code 54.052(b) unless the student qualifies independently under the Texas high school graduation route in 54.052(a)(3).

How to establish residency

  1. Live in Texas for 12 continuous months. Keep one fixed address, and keep proof of it for the whole period.
  2. Show intent to stay. These are the acts this state looks for:
    • Maintain gainful employment in Texas for 12 consecutive months, documented by pay stubs or an employer statement showing beginning and current or ending dates
    • Purchase and live in residential real property in Texas held in sole or joint marital ownership, documented by a filed Warranty Deed showing 12 consecutive months of ownership
    • Own and customarily manage a business organized in Texas that operates regularly, documented by Secretary of State filings, client invoices, business bank statements, and business tax filings
    • Obtain a Texas driver license or Texas identification card
    • Register a vehicle in Texas
    • Register to vote in Texas
    • Open and use Texas bank accounts
    • Sign a residential lease in Texas covering the 12 month period
    • Hold a professional license issued by Texas
    • List a Texas address on federal tax filings
  3. Document your finances. Be ready to prove how you support yourself and that nobody out of state claims you as a dependent.
  4. Cut ties to your old state. Surrender the old license, cancel the old voter registration, and move your vehicle registration.
  5. Petition your university. File the reclassification application with the registrar or residency office before their deadline.

Documents to collect

  • Core Residency Questions form, submitted through ApplyTexas or directly to the institution. At UT Austin this is the online Texas Residency Questionnaire at https://utdirect.utexas.edu/apps/student/residency/
  • Pay stubs covering the 12 consecutive months preceding the census date, or a written employer statement listing dates of employment, hours, and salary
  • Federal tax documents covering at least 12 months for a self employed applicant
  • Filed Warranty Deed showing owner name, address, and purchase date, evidencing 12 consecutive months of Texas residential property ownership
  • Texas Secretary of State business filings, client invoices, business bank statements, and business tax filings covering 12 consecutive months
  • Lease agreements or utility bills covering the past 12 months
  • Texas driver license, Texas vehicle registration, and Texas voter registration
  • Parent federal tax return if claiming dependent status through a parent

Deadlines

The operative date is the census date of the term, which at UT Austin is the twelfth class day in fall and spring and the fourth class day in summer. The 12 months of domicile must be complete as of that date. Requests for reclassification submitted after the census date generally apply to the following term rather than the current one. UT Austin begins reviewing residency requests roughly eight weeks before a semester starts, approximately late May for fall, late October for spring, and late March for summer, and does not publish a hard cutoff separate from the census date, so families should confirm the exact date with the UT Austin residency official for their term.

Shortcuts and exceptions

These groups may qualify faster, or right away:

  • Texas high school graduation route under Texas Education Code 54.052(a)(3): graduate from a Texas high school or earn a Texas GED, maintain residence in Texas for the three years preceding graduation, and reside in Texas the 12 months before the census date of enrollment
  • Active duty military members whose Home of Record is Texas, and qualifying military dependents
  • Veterans using education benefits, subject to UT Austin review
  • Nonresident tuition waivers that let a nonresident pay the resident rate without becoming a resident, including military affiliated waivers and Reserve Officers' Training Corps participation
  • Spouse or parent domicile may be used to satisfy the domicile requirement in place of the student's own

The detail most people miss

19 Texas Administrative Code 21.24 flatly presumes that a person who moved to Texas primarily to attend college as a full time student lacks the intent to make Texas a domicile, and only clear and convincing evidence rebuts it. UT Austin reinforces this by stating it may request any documentation showing a permanent Texas residence established for purposes other than education. A second wrinkle worth flagging: UT Austin tuition is a flat rate that varies by college, so the resident figure runs from about $10,858 to $13,576 and the nonresident figure from about $42,554 to $51,106 for 2026-27, with McCombs, Cockrell Engineering, and Natural Sciences carrying differential tuition. The lower end of each range is reported in the tuition fields above.

Common mistakes

  • Starting late. The clock runs before enrollment, so a plan that begins in your first semester is already behind.
  • Keeping old-state ties. An out-of-state license, voter registration, or car tag can sink an otherwise strong case.
  • Thin documentation. Reviewers decide on paper. If you cannot prove it, it did not happen.
  • Assuming your school reads the rule loosely. Campuses vary in how strictly they apply the same statute.
