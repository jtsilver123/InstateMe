How to Get In-State Tuition in Georgia

Bottom line: Only with a strong case

There is a strong presumption against students who moved here for school. You can still win, but you need clear evidence and usually financial independence. Board of Regents policy bars gaining in-state status while attending a Georgia postsecondary institution absent 'clear evidence of having established domicile in Georgia for purposes other than attending a postsecondary educational institution in this state.' Students under 24 are presumed dependent, so most undergrads can only qualify through a parent who has been domiciled in Georgia for 12 months. The controlling rule is O.C.G.A. § 20-3-66; USG Board of Regents Policy Manual § 04.03.02 (waivers at § 07.03.04.01).

Who can qualify

If you are independent

An independent student is one 'not claimed as a dependent on the federal or state income tax returns of a parent or United States court appointed legal guardian, and whose parent or guardian has ceased to provide support and rights to that individual's care, custody, and earnings.' Students under 24 are presumed dependent unless they provide clear and convincing evidence of financial independence and non-claim as a tax dependent for the most recent tax year; reaching age 18 alone does not qualify a student for reclassification.

If your parents claim you

A dependent student is in-state if a parent (or U.S. court-appointed legal guardian, appointed before the student turned 18 and not for tuition-avoidance purposes) has maintained Georgia domicile for at least 12 consecutive months immediately preceding the first day of classes AND the student either graduated from a Georgia high school or was claimed as a dependent on the parent's most recent federal or state return.

How to establish residency

  1. Live in Georgia for 12 continuous months. Keep one fixed address, and keep proof of it for the whole period.
  2. Show intent to stay. These are the acts this state looks for:
    • Georgia driver's license or state-issued ID
    • Georgia vehicle registration
    • Georgia voter registration
    • Georgia income tax return (Form 500/500EZ) filed with the GA Dept. of Revenue
    • lease or deed for a Georgia residence
    • employment verification on company letterhead / W-2s / pay stubs
    • Georgia property tax receipts and homestead exemption documents
    • Georgia business or professional license
    • utility bills and bank statements with a Georgia address
  3. Document your finances. Be ready to prove how you support yourself and that nobody out of state claims you as a dependent.
  4. Cut ties to your old state. Surrender the old license, cancel the old voter registration, and move your vehicle registration.
  5. Petition your university. File the reclassification application with the registrar or residency office before their deadline.

Documents to collect

  • Georgia income tax return or state tax transcript (current and prior year)
  • lease or deed for a Georgia residence (notarized statement if in another person's name)
  • Georgia driver's license or state ID
  • Georgia vehicle registration
  • Georgia voter registration
  • W-2s, pay stubs or LES and official employment verification
  • federal tax return or transcript showing dependency status
  • court documents for U.S. court-appointed guardianship
  • signed statement of reason for moving to Georgia

Deadlines

UGA petitions should be received by August 1 (fall), December 1 (spring) and May 1 (summer) to be decided before the tuition payment deadline; petitions are never considered for previous terms. Dates come from a form revised 11/14/2022, confirm with regresidency@uga.edu.

Shortcuts and exceptions

These groups may qualify faster, or right away:

  • active-duty military and qualifying federal service members, spouses and dependent children (20 U.S.C. § 1015d(d))
  • Georgia National Guard and Georgia-based U.S. Military Reserve members, spouses and dependents
  • separated service members within 36 months of separation and transferred-VA-benefit users (38 U.S.C. § 3679(c))
  • Economic Advantage Waiver (family relocated to Georgia for full-time self-sustaining employment)
  • Border County and Border Resident waivers
  • full-time USG employees, spouses and dependent children
  • full-time Georgia public school and TCSG employees, spouses and dependents
  • Academic Common Market and approved exchange/study-abroad participants
  • career consular officials stationed in Georgia, spouses and dependent children
  • Presidential waivers (academic, athletic, international) subject to enrollment caps
  • students from a homeless situation
  • Non-Resident Student waiver via a Georgia-domiciled parent, guardian or spouse

The detail most people miss

Georgia's Economic Advantage Waiver, for families who relocate to Georgia for full-time, self-sustaining employment, 'expires twelve (12) months from the date the waiver is awarded' for citizens and permanent residents. It is deliberately a one-year bridge to cover the 12-month durational requirement, not a four-year solution, and families who treat it as permanent get a bill in year two.

Common mistakes

  • Starting late. The clock runs before enrollment, so a plan that begins in your first semester is already behind.
  • Keeping old-state ties. An out-of-state license, voter registration, or car tag can sink an otherwise strong case.
  • Thin documentation. Reviewers decide on paper. If you cannot prove it, it did not happen.
  • Assuming your school reads the rule loosely. Campuses vary in how strictly they apply the same statute.
