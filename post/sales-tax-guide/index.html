Texas Sales Tax Guide for Your LLC

By Jake Silver·Feb 14, 2025·3 min read·Updated Oct 21, 2025

This step-by-step guide is written for InstateMe clients working through their personalized residency checklist — but it’s free for everyone. Questions? Email us or book a free call.

Disclaimer: this guide is for general informational purposes only and is not legal or tax advice. For specific questions about your business’s sales tax obligations, consult a qualified tax professional or contact the Texas Comptroller (800-252-5555).

1. Understanding sales tax in Texas

What is sales tax? A tax imposed on the sale of tangible personal property and certain services. In Texas, businesses must collect sales tax on taxable sales and remit it to the state.

Which goods and services are taxable?

  • Tangible personal property: most physical products — electronics, clothing, home goods, office supplies. If you’re selling a physical product, it’s likely taxable unless a specific exemption applies.
  • Certain services: not all services are taxable, but Texas does tax some — check the Texas Comptroller’s guidelines.
  • Exemptions: sales to resellers (with a valid resale certificate), certain food products, prescription medicines and medical devices, and specific goods outlined by state law.

Rates: the state rate is 6.25%; local jurisdictions can add up to 2% (Austin does), for a combined rate as high as 8.25%. Use the Comptroller’s online tools to determine the right rate for a sale’s location.

2. Registering for a Sales Tax Permit

Any business selling taxable goods or services in Texas must register for a Sales Tax Permit. Register online at the Comptroller’s website (“Apply for a Sales Tax Permit”) with your business name and address, EIN, business structure, and estimated monthly sales. There is no fee to register.

3. Collecting and remitting sales tax

  • Collect: add the appropriate state and local sales tax to each taxable sale.
  • File: monthly, quarterly, or annually depending on your sales volume; returns are generally due the 20th of the month after your reporting period ends.
  • Remit via Webfile: go to comptroller.texas.gov → “Webfile eSystems Login” → E-systems Dashboard → My Taxpayer Accounts: Sales and Use Tax → File Original Return. Choose the return dates, answer the credits/refund questions (usually “No”), enter Total Texas Sales, Taxable Sales (sales where tax wasn’t collected by a third-party platform like Etsy), and Taxable Purchases (usually 0). Pay by electronic check to avoid fees.

4. Recordkeeping

Maintain detailed records of all sales transactions, tax collected, and any exemption certificates — crucial for managing your business and in the event of an audit. Keep sales receipts and invoices, records of collected sales tax, and copies of resale or exemption certificates.

5. Additional considerations

  • Online sales: if you sell online and ship within Texas, you must collect Texas sales tax provided you have nexus (a business presence) in the state.
  • Staying updated: laws and rates change — check the Comptroller’s office regularly.
  • Professional advice: for unique circumstances, consult a tax professional.

6. Summary

  1. Register for a Sales Tax Permit (free, online).
  2. Collect the correct state + local tax on each sale.
  3. File returns on time via Webfile and remit the tax.
  4. Keep detailed records.
