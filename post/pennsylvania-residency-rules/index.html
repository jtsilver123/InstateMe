How to Get In-State Tuition in Pennsylvania

InstateMe research·Jul 10, 2026

Difficulty
Hard
Time required
12 monthsA student who has continuously resided in Pennsylvania for purposes other than education for at least 12 consecutive months immediately preceding their initial enrollment at the University or at an institution of higher education is presumed to have a Pennsylvania domicile. A student who has not done so is presumed not to be a Pennsylvania resident and may rebut that presumption only by clear and convincing evidence.

Bottom line: Check the exceptions first

There is a structural barrier here, not just a high bar. For a typical dependent undergraduate the standard route is effectively closed, so the exceptions further down are the realistic path. The qualifying year is measured against initial enrollment in higher education, so it cannot accrue while the student is attending. Penn State's Office of the Bursar states plainly that the 12 month requirement cannot be met while attending Penn State because the student is assumed to be in the Commonwealth for educational purposes, and that a student who lives in Pennsylvania for a year while enrolled remains a non resident absent clear evidence of changed circumstances and a permanent independent domicile. Layered on top is a presumption that any undergraduate under 24 holds the parents' domicile. The controlling rule is Pennsylvania has no single statewide residency statute for public university tuition. Each institution sets its own policy. Penn State Policy FN36, Residency Classification for Tuition Purposes..

Who can qualify

If you are independent

An undergraduate under the age of 24 is presumed to have the domicile of the parents or court appointed guardians and may rebut that presumption only with clear and convincing evidence of financial emancipation. Proof typically includes the student's and the parents' federal tax returns showing the student is not claimed as a dependent, or sworn statements from the parents or legal guardians setting out facts establishing the student's financial independence.

If your parents claim you

If the parents of an undergraduate under 24 are domiciled in Pennsylvania, the student is presumed to be domiciled in Pennsylvania and receives in state tuition through the parents' domicile. This is by far the most common route to the in state rate.

How to establish residency

  1. Live in Pennsylvania for 12 continuous months. Keep one fixed address, and keep proof of it for the whole period.
  2. Show intent to stay. These are the acts this state looks for:
    • Lease or purchase of a permanent, independent residence in Pennsylvania
    • Obtaining a Pennsylvania driver's license
    • Registering to vote in Pennsylvania
    • Payment of applicable state and local taxes and filing of the appropriate returns
    • Transfer of vehicle registration to Pennsylvania
    • Transfer of other property registrations to Pennsylvania
    • Permanent, non student employment in Pennsylvania
  3. Document your finances. Be ready to prove how you support yourself and that nobody out of state claims you as a dependent.
  4. Cut ties to your old state. Surrender the old license, cancel the old voter registration, and move your vehicle registration.
  5. Petition your university. File the reclassification application with the registrar or residency office before their deadline.

Documents to collect

  • Lease or deed for a permanent independent Pennsylvania residence
  • Pennsylvania and prior state tax returns for the student and, where relevant, the parents
  • Pennsylvania voter registration
  • Pennsylvania driver's license
  • Pennsylvania vehicle registration
  • Employment verification for permanent non student employment
  • Sworn parental statements establishing financial emancipation
  • DD-214 discharge papers or military Leave and Earnings Statements where applicable

Deadlines

Residency classification is set at admission and reviewed on petition through the Office of the Bursar's residency review process. An appeal of a written residency decision must be submitted within 30 days of the date of that decision. No residency review is considered after the student has graduated.

Shortcuts and exceptions

These groups may qualify faster, or right away:

  • Members of the armed forces stationed in Pennsylvania are deemed Pennsylvania residents for tuition purposes
  • Students using Department of Veterans Affairs education benefits receive in state tuition regardless of residency classification
  • Non citizen undergraduates who attended an accredited Pennsylvania high school for at least four years and graduated or earned a GED may qualify, provided they are lawfully present and not on an F-1 or J-1 student visa
  • Non citizen graduate students who earned a baccalaureate degree from a regionally accredited Pennsylvania institution may qualify
  • F-1 and J-1 student visa holders are excluded from Pennsylvania resident classification

The detail most people miss

Pennsylvania has no uniform statewide rule, so Penn State, Pitt, Temple and the PASSHE universities each apply their own standard, and a family's answer can differ by campus. Penn State's own version is one of the strictest in the country because the 12 month clock is pegged to initial enrollment at any institution of higher education, not just Penn State, so transferring in from an out of state community college does not restart it.

Common mistakes

  • Starting late. The clock runs before enrollment, so a plan that begins in your first semester is already behind.
  • Keeping old-state ties. An out-of-state license, voter registration, or car tag can sink an otherwise strong case.
  • Thin documentation. Reviewers decide on paper. If you cannot prove it, it did not happen.
  • Assuming your school reads the rule loosely. Campuses vary in how strictly they apply the same statute.
