Submitting Your Residency Application

By Jake Silver·Jan 17, 2023·3 min read·Updated Nov 13, 2025

This step-by-step guide is written for InstateMe clients working through their personalized residency checklist — but it’s free for everyone. Questions? Email us or book a free call.

To ensure proper determination of residency qualification for all Texas schools, you must submit a Core Residency Questionnaire. Depending on your institution, you will either email the completed form to the residency office or fill out an online form. This form, coupled with PDFs of documentation, is the evidence you will provide to make your residency case.

Step-by-step guide

  1. View the final combined PDF in your folder.
  2. Access the form for your school (Texas A&M and UT Austin each publish their own version of the Core Residency Questions).
  3. Part A — Student basic information: full name, student ID, date of birth, and your current (Texas) address.
  4. Part B — Previous enrollment: yes, you attended a public college in the fall or spring term; enter your Texas public institution, the last term enrolled, and that you paid nonresident (out-of-state) tuition. Skip question 5.
  5. Part C — Residency claim: yes, you are a resident of Texas.
  6. Part D — High school diploma or GED: no, you didn’t graduate from a Texas high school; no, you didn’t live in Texas the 36 months before graduation; yes, you will have lived in Texas at least 12 months; yes, you are a U.S. citizen.
  7. Part E — Basis of claim: yes, you filed your own taxes; no, you are not a dependent; skip question 3.
  8. Part F: yes, you are a citizen; no, you are not a permanent resident (or “n/a”); no, you are not a foreign national; skip question 4; yes, you currently live in Texas; enter how long you’ve been in Texas (at least 12 months in most cases — counted from the first day you moved for freshman year; if submitting at month 11, it’s okay to say 12 months, and if that’s an issue they will correct you).
    • Reason for being in Texas: most students choose “Establish/maintain a home.” Per Texas Administrative Code §21.24(g), an individual whose initial purpose for moving to Texas is to attend school full-time is presumed not to have the required intent — but the presumption may be overruled by clear and convincing evidence.
    • Question 8a: if you went the property route, yes; otherwise no. 8b: if you went the business route, yes — the date acquired is the date the LLC was formed (top right of your Certificate of Formation, where it says “Filing”).
    • Question 9a: if you went the employment route, yes; otherwise no (if you have an LLC, 9a is “no”). 9b: no.
    • Question 10a: no, you are not married; skip 10b and 10c.
  9. Skip Part G; fill out Part H (see our residency essay guide).
  10. Submit — try the portal first (upload the file after clicking submit); otherwise email the final application with your combined PDF to the residency office (UT Austin: residency@austin.utexas.edu, including your student ID and full name). Compress the file if it’s too large.
  11. After submitting, update your address in your student portal to the primary address your LLC was registered to (found on your Articles of Organization).
  12. Continue to operate your business, and wait about 3 weeks to be reclassified as a resident.
  13. When you hear back, email jsilver@instateme.com with your residency outcome. If they require more info, we’ll work on it together — don’t sweat it. Then pay the savings fee… or be our first-ever refund. (That wouldn’t be fun.)
