How to Get In-State Tuition in Wyoming

Bottom line: Only with a strong case

There is a strong presumption against students who moved here for school. You can still win, but you need clear evidence and usually financial independence. UW Regulation 2-200 states flatly that 'students residing in Wyoming primarily as a student are not eligible for resident status for tuition purposes,' and requires 5 of 8 domicile criteria each completed at least 12 consecutive months before petitioning. The extenuating-circumstances route additionally requires proof of a non-academic reason for being in-state. The controlling rule is Wyo. Stat. § 21-17-105; University of Wyoming Regulation 2-200 (Student Classification for In-State and Non-Resident Tuition), approved Oct. 13, 2021.

Who can qualify

If you are independent

Not explicitly required as a standalone self-support test; UW Regulation 2-200 sets no dependency or percentage threshold for the domicile route. The practical constraint is inside the 'Gainful Employment' criterion, which requires documented Wyoming employment or self-employment of at least 20 hours per week continuously for at least 12 months, and expressly provides that 'employment and monies derived dependent upon a status as a student does not qualify', so work-study, assistantships and other student-status jobs are excluded.

If your parents claim you

A financial dependent under the age of 24 who is under the care, custody and support of a parent or legal guardian who is a Wyoming resident under the regulation is classified as a resident.

How to establish residency

  1. Live in Wyoming for 12 continuous months. Keep one fixed address, and keep proof of it for the whole period.
  2. Show intent to stay. These are the acts this state looks for:
    • Wyoming vehicle registration
    • valid Wyoming driver's license or government-issued photo ID
    • gainful employment in Wyoming
    • Wyoming voter registration
    • Wyoming address on the most recent federal income tax return
    • documented use of a local Wyoming bank
    • change of permanent address on all Wyoming records
    • proof of receiving public assistance from a Wyoming state, city, county or municipal agency
  3. Cut ties to your old state. Surrender the old license, cancel the old voter registration, and move your vehicle registration.
  4. Petition your university. File the reclassification application with the registrar or residency office before their deadline.

Documents to collect

  • Wyoming vehicle registration
  • Wyoming driver's license or state photo ID
  • employment records showing 20+ hours per week for 12 months
  • voter registration
  • most recent federal income tax return showing a Wyoming address
  • Wyoming bank statements
  • proof of permanent address change on Wyoming records
  • documentation of public assistance from a Wyoming agency

Deadlines

Petitions must be submitted to the University Registrar on or before the first day of classes for the semester; submit three weeks before the start of the semester for evaluation before classes begin. Reclassification is never applied retroactively.

Shortcuts and exceptions

These groups may qualify faster, or right away:

  • graduates of a Wyoming high school
  • active Wyoming National Guard members and U.S. Armed Forces members stationed in Wyoming, and their dependents
  • graduate students for the duration of a University-funded or externally sponsored fellowship or graduate assistantship
  • Wyoming residents temporarily absent for military service, education or other documented temporary absence
  • holders of an associate's degree from a Wyoming community college who had resident status there and enroll at UW within one year
  • financial dependents under 24 of a Wyoming resident, and financial dependents who are the spouse of a Wyoming resident
  • veterans, eligible individuals and covered individuals under 38 U.S.C. § 3679(c)(2)
  • reciprocal residency for cross-border workers under Wyo. Stat. § 21-17-105(d)
  • Hathaway scholarship recipients qualify for resident tuition (Wyo. Stat. § 21-17-105(h))
  • Western Undergraduate Exchange award at 150% of resident tuition (a discount, not residency)

The detail most people miss

Wyoming has a reciprocal-residency statute that can deliver in-state tuition to a family that does not live in Wyoming at all. Under Wyo. Stat. § 21-17-105(d), a person, 'including the spouse and any child of that person', qualifies as a resident for tuition if their principal employment is in Wyoming and is the family's principal income source, they pay Wyoming taxes, they have been employed in Wyoming for a majority of the preceding 12 months, they live in a state with a substantially similar reciprocal law, and they file a notarized affidavit with the university. Separately, note there is no Wyoming state income tax, so a state return is not available as proof, the regulation asks instead for a Wyoming address on the FEDERAL return.

Common mistakes

  • Starting late. The clock runs before enrollment, so a plan that begins in your first semester is already behind.
  • Keeping old-state ties. An out-of-state license, voter registration, or car tag can sink an otherwise strong case.
  • Thin documentation. Reviewers decide on paper. If you cannot prove it, it did not happen.
  • Assuming your school reads the rule loosely. Campuses vary in how strictly they apply the same statute.
