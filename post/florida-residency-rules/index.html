How to Get In-State Tuition in Florida

Bottom line: Only with a strong case

There is a strong presumption against students who moved here for school. You can still win, but you need clear evidence and usually financial independence. A student claimed as a dependent under the federal tax code is bound to the parent's residency, so the parent rather than the student must prove 12 months of Florida domicile. Most out-of-state undergraduates cannot qualify on their own. Reclassification also demands clear and convincing evidence, three qualifying documents, and proof you gave up legal residency in any other state. The controlling rule is Fla. Stat. Section 1009.21.

Who can qualify

If you are independent

No separate self-support percentage test. Dependency is decided by the federal tax code: if a parent can claim you, the parent's residency controls. You are independent if you are 24 or older by the first day of class, married, have dependents of your own, are a veteran or active duty, were a ward of the court or in foster care until 18, are an unaccompanied homeless youth, or are pursuing a graduate degree in Florida.

If your parents claim you

The parent must have maintained Florida legal residence for 12 consecutive months before the first day of classes and supplies the qualifying documents. A dependent who has lived continuously with a Florida-resident adult relative other than a parent for at least three years may document through that relative instead.

How to establish residency

  1. Live in Florida for 12 continuous months. Keep one fixed address, and keep proof of it for the whole period.
  2. Show intent to stay. These are the acts this state looks for:
    • Florida driver's license or state ID card
    • Florida vehicle registration
    • Florida voter registration
    • A declaration of domicile filed with the Clerk of the Circuit Court
    • Homestead exemption on a Florida home
    • Proof of a permanent Florida home occupied as your primary residence
    • Permanent full-time Florida employment of 30 or more hours a week for 12 months
    • Twelve consecutive months of Florida lease and utility payments
  3. Document your finances. Be ready to prove how you support yourself and that nobody out of state claims you as a dependent.
  4. Cut ties to your old state. Surrender the old license, cancel the old voter registration, and move your vehicle registration.
  5. Petition your university. File the reclassification application with the registrar or residency office before their deadline.

Documents to collect

  • First tier, at least one required: Florida voter registration card, driver license, state ID card, vehicle registration, proof of a permanent Florida home occupied as a primary residence, proof of a Florida homestead exemption, transcripts from a Florida high school, or proof of permanent full-time Florida employment for 12 months
  • Second tier, one or more: declaration of domicile, Florida professional or occupational license, Florida incorporation, documented family ties to a Florida resident, membership in a Florida charitable or professional organization, utility bills with 12 consecutive months of payments, or a lease with 12 consecutive months of payments
  • Florida Residency Declaration form
  • Not accepted: hunting or fishing license, library card, shopping club card, birth certificate, passport, Social Security card, concealed weapons permit, or insurance card

Deadlines

At the University of Florida, reclassification requests and all supporting documents are due by the fee payment deadline for the term you want in-state status in. Reclassification is never granted retroactively for prior terms.

Shortcuts and exceptions

These groups may qualify faster, or right away:

  • Active-duty military stationed or residing in Florida, plus spouses and dependent children
  • Active drilling members of the Florida National Guard
  • Military whose home of record is Florida, plus spouse and dependents
  • Marriage to a Florida legal resident who meets the 12-month requirement
  • Full-time teaching and administrative staff of Florida public schools and universities, plus spouse and dependents
  • Honorably discharged veterans using the out-of-state fee waiver, with no 12-month wait, only physical residence while enrolled
  • Qualified beneficiaries of the Florida Prepaid College Program
  • SREB Academic Common Market graduate students
  • A one-time re-enrollment benefit for a former Florida resident who re-establishes domicile within 12 months
  • A 12-month grace period for enrolled students whose parents move out of state

The detail most people miss

Filing a declaration of domicile does not backdate anything. The 12-month clock starts on the date the Clerk of the Circuit Court notes it was sworn, so a student who files on arrival waits a full year from that date. Note also the asymmetry: initial classification takes two documents, but reclassification from nonresident to resident requires a minimum of three. At the opposite extreme, proof of a Florida homestead exemption is treated by statute as conclusive evidence that stands alone.

Common mistakes

  • Starting late. The clock runs before enrollment, so a plan that begins in your first semester is already behind.
  • Keeping old-state ties. An out-of-state license, voter registration, or car tag can sink an otherwise strong case.
  • Thin documentation. Reviewers decide on paper. If you cannot prove it, it did not happen.
  • Assuming your school reads the rule loosely. Campuses vary in how strictly they apply the same statute.
