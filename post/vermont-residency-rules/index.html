How to Get In-State Tuition in Vermont

InstateMe research·Jul 10, 2026

Difficulty
Medium
Time required
12 months12 months continuous Vermont residence plus separately established domicile
Flagship school
University of Vermont
Savings per year
$29,7172026-27 published rates

Bottom line: Worth pursuing with planning

Winnable, but you will need to prove financial independence and keep tight documentation for the whole qualifying period. UVM's Board of Trustees regulations state that 'a residence or domicile established for the purpose of attending UVM shall not qualify a student for in-state status,' and presume that a student who entered as out-of-state remains in Vermont for education; rebuttal requires clear and convincing evidence. Receipt of any family financial support creates a further presumption of the family's domicile 'regardless of whether the student has reached the age of 18.' The controlling rule is 16 V.S.A. § 2282a (note: 16 V.S.A. § 2282 was repealed by 2015, No. 172 (Adj. Sess.), § E.600.1).

Who can qualify

If you are independent

A 'financially independent student' is one age 18+ or an emancipated minor whose predominant source of income is their own earnings, investments, grants, scholarships or commercial loans. The applicant must certify they have not been and will not be claimed as a tax exemption by anyone other than self or spouse, and have not received cash or in-kind support at or above the dependency-exemption threshold, for BOTH the current calendar year and the calendar year immediately before, effectively a two-tax-year clean window, not twelve months.

If your parents claim you

A dependent student qualifies on the domicile of a parent or court-appointed legal guardian who has maintained bona fide Vermont domicile for at least one year; where parents are separated or divorced, a non-custodial or joint-custodial Vermont parent works if they contributed more than 50 percent of support for at least one year.

How to establish residency

  1. Live in Vermont for 12 continuous months. Keep one fixed address, and keep proof of it for the whole period.
  2. Show intent to stay. These are the acts this state looks for:
    • Vermont driver's license or non-driver ID card
    • Vermont voter registration
    • Vermont motor vehicle registration
    • Vermont resident income tax return
    • employment in Vermont
    • property owned or leased in Vermont
    • Vermont professional licenses
    • absence from Vermont of no more than 30 days at a time
  3. Document your finances. Be ready to prove how you support yourself and that nobody out of state claims you as a dependent.
  4. Cut ties to your old state. Surrender the old license, cancel the old voter registration, and move your vehicle registration.
  5. Petition your university. File the reclassification application with the registrar or residency office before their deadline.

Documents to collect

  • signed and current lease covering the full 12 consecutive months, or a notarized landlord letter, or deed/property tax bill
  • first AND most recent statement for each utility or service (electric, gas, water, phone, insurance, bank)
  • federal Form 1040 with proof of filing plus complete state returns
  • parents' federal and state returns if the student is 24 or under
  • Vermont driver's license or ID card showing date of issue
  • vehicle registration
  • voter registration
  • W-2s
  • proof of citizenship or permanent residency
  • written narrative addressing each regulation

Deadlines

By 11:59 PM on the last day of the Add/Drop period for the semester for which in-state status is sought; applications may be filed as early as 75 days before the first day of classes. Never applied retroactively.

Shortcuts and exceptions

These groups may qualify faster, or right away:

  • active duty U.S. Armed Forces transferred to Vermont for non-educational duty (16 V.S.A. § 2282a(b))
  • spouse and dependent children of a servicemember stationed in Vermont, retained even after transfer out or retirement if they keep residing in Vermont
  • veterans using Montgomery GI Bill, Post-9/11 GI Bill, Fry Scholarship, VR&E or DEA (38 U.S.C. § 3679(c))
  • Vermont National Guard Tuition Benefit Program (16 V.S.A. § 2857)
  • NEBHE New England Regional Student Program tuition break at 175% of in-state for CT, MA, ME, NH, RI residents in approved majors (a discount, not residency)

The detail most people miss

Parental money defeats a claim at ANY age, UVM Regulation 7 makes receipt of family financial support a rebuttable presumption that the student's domicile is the family's, 'regardless of whether the student has reached the age of 18.' There is no age cutoff, so a 27-year-old who has lived and worked in Burlington for years but whose out-of-state parents still cover part of the rent or a phone bill starts out presumed non-resident.

Common mistakes

  • Starting late. The clock runs before enrollment, so a plan that begins in your first semester is already behind.
  • Keeping old-state ties. An out-of-state license, voter registration, or car tag can sink an otherwise strong case.
  • Thin documentation. Reviewers decide on paper. If you cannot prove it, it did not happen.
  • Assuming your school reads the rule loosely. Campuses vary in how strictly they apply the same statute.
