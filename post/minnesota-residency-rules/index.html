How to Get In-State Tuition in Minnesota

Bottom line: Check the exceptions first

There is a structural barrier here, not just a high bar. For a typical dependent undergraduate the standard route is effectively closed, so the exceptions further down are the realistic path. The University of Minnesota's basic definition requires one calendar year of continuous presence and adds: 'During that one year period, the student must not have attended any Minnesota post-secondary educational institutions.' The handbook further states that a student who moves to Minnesota and attends another post-secondary institution within that first year 'will be classified as a nonresident and shall remain a nonresident throughout his or her presence as a student,' which effectively closes the door once you enroll. The controlling rule is University of Minnesota Board of Regents / administrative policy (no general Minnesota residency-for-tuition statute governs the University). Minn. Stat. 135A.043 covers only Minnesota high-school-attendance/graduation-based eligibility (the Minnesota Dream Act pathway), not general domicile..

Who can qualify

If you are independent

Dependent students are 'those who are receiving 50 percent or more of their total support from parent(s) and/or legal guardian(s) and are claimed as tax dependents on their parents' or guardians' federal tax return.' Dependent students acquire the resident status of their parents or guardians rather than establishing their own.

If your parents claim you

A dependent student takes the resident status of the parent or guardian; if a Minnesota resident parent later moves out of state, the dependent student remains eligible for resident status for at least one year thereafter. In separated/divorced families, a student living out of state may still qualify if one parent meets the Minnesota residency definition and provides 50 percent or more of the student's total support.

How to establish residency

  1. Live in Minnesota for 12 continuous months. Keep one fixed address, and keep proof of it for the whole period.
  2. Show intent to stay. These are the acts this state looks for:
    • one calendar year of continuous physical presence in Minnesota without attending a Minnesota post-secondary institution
    • Minnesota driver's license or state ID
    • Minnesota tax return
    • employment in Minnesota (letter from employer with dates and hours)
    • Minnesota property tax statement or first Minnesota lease
    • Minnesota voter registration
    • closing documents on a previous out-of-state residence
  3. Document your finances. Be ready to prove how you support yourself and that nobody out of state claims you as a dependent.
  4. Cut ties to your old state. Surrender the old license, cancel the old voter registration, and move your vehicle registration.
  5. Petition your university. File the reclassification application with the registrar or residency office before their deadline.

Documents to collect

  • Resident Classification Evaluation Request form
  • letter from the supporting parent or guardian stating how long they have lived in Minnesota, whether they provide 50 percent or more of support, and whether they claim the student as a federal tax dependent
  • Minnesota driver's license or state ID
  • last Minnesota tax return
  • letter from employer with dates of employment and hours worked
  • first Minnesota lease agreement or property tax statement
  • closing documents on a previous non-Minnesota residence
  • Minnesota voter registration
  • DD-214 or military dependent ID
  • divorce/custody and support documents

Deadlines

The Resident Classification Evaluation Request form must be submitted no later than May 1 of the same academic year in which resident status is sought. Resident status may be applied retroactively only to the current academic year, with any refund of the nonresident portion capped at one academic year's tuition. Reciprocity should be applied for before the first semester of enrollment.

Shortcuts and exceptions

These groups may qualify faster, or right away:

  • Minnesota-Wisconsin tuition reciprocity
  • Minnesota-North Dakota tuition reciprocity
  • Minnesota-Manitoba (Canada) tuition reciprocity
  • Minnesota-South Dakota reciprocity (ENDED - new South Dakota students pay nonresident rates beginning fall 2025; continuing students grandfathered)
  • graduates of Minnesota high schools who enroll within two calendar years
  • Minnesota Dream Act / DACA students (apply via the Minnesota Office of Higher Education)
  • active-duty military stationed in Minnesota, plus immediate family
  • full-time faculty at accredited Minnesota colleges
  • permanent residents of migrant background employed in Minnesota seasonal agricultural labor for at least one cumulative year in the past five
  • accredited foreign diplomatic officials and immediate family
  • students whose tuition is paid by the State of Minnesota

The detail most people miss

Minnesota's real in-state pathway for most out-of-state families is reciprocity, not residency - and the map just changed. South Dakota's reciprocity agreement ended: students newly admitted from South Dakota pay full nonresident rates beginning fall 2025, while those admitted fall 2024 through spring 2025 keep Minnesota resident rates if they maintain continuous degree progress. Wisconsin, North Dakota and Manitoba reciprocity remain, but it 'is not automatic' - you must apply through your own state's higher education office, not through the University.

Common mistakes

  • Starting late. The clock runs before enrollment, so a plan that begins in your first semester is already behind.
  • Keeping old-state ties. An out-of-state license, voter registration, or car tag can sink an otherwise strong case.
  • Thin documentation. Reviewers decide on paper. If you cannot prove it, it did not happen.
  • Assuming your school reads the rule loosely. Campuses vary in how strictly they apply the same statute.
