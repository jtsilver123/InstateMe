How to Get In-State Tuition in South Carolina

InstateMe research·Jul 10, 2026

Difficulty
Medium
Time required
12 monthsIndependent persons who reside in and have been domiciled in South Carolina for no less than twelve months with the intention of making a permanent home there, and their dependents, may be considered eligible for in state rates. When domicile is taken in South Carolina after enrollment, the student does not become eligible for in state rates until the beginning of the next academic session after twelve months from the date of domicile.
Flagship school
University of South Carolina, Columbia
Savings per year
$25,8122026-27 published rates

Bottom line: Worth pursuing with planning

Winnable, but you will need to prove financial independence and keep tight documentation for the whole qualifying period. There is a strong presumption against students plus a heavy documentary burden, but no absolute structural bar. Section 59-112-30(A) expressly contemplates that domicile can change after enrollment and that in state rates then begin after twelve months, so the clock can run while a student is enrolled. However, USC applies the regulation that resident status may not be acquired while residing in South Carolina primarily for enrollment, requires the student to have provided 51 percent or more of their own support for the previous twelve months, and requires a South Carolina driver's license and vehicle registration within 45 days of establishing domicile. Section 59-112-80 places the burden of proof on the student. The controlling rule is S.C. Code Ann. Title 59, Chapter 112 (Determination of Rates of Tuition and Fees), particularly Sections 59-112-10, 59-112-20, 59-112-30, 59-112-50 and 59-112-80..

Who can qualify

If you are independent

An independent person is a person in their majority, or an emancipated minor, whose predominant source of income is their own earnings or income from employment, investments, trusts, grants, scholarships, loans, or court ordered alimony or separate maintenance. USC additionally requires the applicant to have provided 51 percent or more of their own financial support toward all expenses, including tuition, for the previous twelve months.

If your parents claim you

A dependent person is one whose predominant source of support is payments from a parent, spouse, or guardian and who qualifies as a dependent or exemption on that person's federal return. Dependents of an independent person who is domiciled in South Carolina for twelve months qualify for in state rates. The residence and domicile of a dependent minor is presumed to be that of the parent. A guardianship created primarily to confer South Carolina domicile is disregarded.

How to establish residency

  1. Live in South Carolina for 12 continuous months. Keep one fixed address, and keep proof of it for the whole period.
  2. Show intent to stay. These are the acts this state looks for:
    • Establish domicile as a true, fixed, principal residence and place of habitation in South Carolina
    • Obtain a South Carolina driver's license within 45 days of establishing domicile
    • Register all vehicles in South Carolina within 45 days of establishing domicile
    • File and pay South Carolina income taxes
    • Full time or permanent employment in South Carolina
    • Ownership of property in South Carolina
    • Holding a South Carolina professional license
  3. Document your finances. Be ready to prove how you support yourself and that nobody out of state claims you as a dependent.
  4. Cut ties to your old state. Surrender the old license, cancel the old voter registration, and move your vehicle registration.
  5. Petition your university. File the reclassification application with the registrar or residency office before their deadline.

Documents to collect

  • Residency Certification Form filed with the University Registrar
  • South Carolina driver's license issued within 45 days of establishing domicile
  • South Carolina vehicle registration issued within 45 days of establishing domicile
  • South Carolina income tax returns
  • Proof of employment or an employer statement
  • Lease, deed, or other proof of maintained domicile
  • Documentation showing 51 percent or more self support for the previous twelve months
  • Parent tax returns where a dependent pathway is claimed

Deadlines

USC priority processing deadlines are July 1 for fall, December 1 for spring, and May 1 for summer. The Residency Certification Form and all supporting documents must be submitted no later than the first day of class for the term in which resident status is sought. Forms received after that are held for the following semester. The twelve month domicile period and the 45 day license and vehicle actions must all be complete before the first day of class for the requested term.

Shortcuts and exceptions

These groups may qualify faster, or right away:

  • Section 59-112-20(B): independent persons domiciled in South Carolina for fewer than twelve months who hold full time employment in the State, and their dependents, are eligible for in state rates for as long as that full time employment continues
  • Section 59-112-30(C): a dependent domiciled with family in South Carolina for at least three years before enrollment keeps in state rates even if the parent later moves out of state
  • Section 59-112-50(A): armed services members stationed in South Carolina and their dependents receive in state rates during the assignment, with continuation protections after reassignment or discharge
  • Section 59-112-50(C): covered veterans and GI Bill beneficiaries who live in the State receive in state rates without regard to length of residence
  • Section 59-112-20(E): residents of Chatham, Effingham and Bryan Counties, Georgia are eligible while Georgia's reciprocal program continues
  • Section 59-112-60: full time faculty and administrative employees of state institutions, their spouses and children, are excluded from the chapter

The detail most people miss

The full time employment shortcut in Section 59-112-20(B) is the standout feature. An independent person with genuine full time South Carolina employment, and their dependents, can receive in state rates immediately with no twelve month wait, but only for as long as that full time employment lasts. Separately, the statute presumes that housing provided on an academic session basis at a state institution is not a place of principal residence, so living in a dorm affirmatively works against a domicile claim, and Section 59-112-90 imposes back tuition at the out of state rate plus 8 percent interest and a 25 percent penalty for willful misrepresentation, with transcripts and graduation withheld until paid.

Common mistakes

  • Starting late. The clock runs before enrollment, so a plan that begins in your first semester is already behind.
  • Keeping old-state ties. An out-of-state license, voter registration, or car tag can sink an otherwise strong case.
  • Thin documentation. Reviewers decide on paper. If you cannot prove it, it did not happen.
  • Assuming your school reads the rule loosely. Campuses vary in how strictly they apply the same statute.
