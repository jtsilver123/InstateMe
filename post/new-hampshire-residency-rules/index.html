Bottom line: Check the exceptions first

For a typical dependent undergraduate, the standard route is effectively closed. The exceptions further down are the realistic path here. System policy bars any un-emancipated student from in-state status unless the parents are domiciled in New Hampshire, and emancipation requires proving the parent entirely surrendered the right to your care, custody, and earnings, with all five tests met. Students are only presumed emancipated at 24, so a typical dependent undergraduate cannot qualify. The controlling rule is USNH Board of Trustees Policy BOT.IV.E, Classification of Students for Tuition Purposes.

Who can qualify

If you are independent

There is no dollar self-support test, but anyone under 24 is presumed un-emancipated, and proving emancipation requires showing all of the following: no parental financial support, no parental contribution to your income, no dependency claim on a parent's tax return, a separate domicile from your parent, and not returning to the parent's home during vacations.

If your parents claim you

An un-emancipated student can get in-state tuition only if the parent, guardian, or person who claims them has been domiciled in New Hampshire for the 12 consecutive months before registration.

How to establish residency

  1. Live in New Hampshire for 12 continuous months. Keep one fixed address, and keep proof of it for the whole period.
  2. Show intent to stay. These are the acts this state looks for:
    • Pay New Hampshire state or local taxes
    • Report New Hampshire as your residence on tax returns
    • Register your car in New Hampshire
    • Obtain a New Hampshire driver's license
    • Stop receiving support from parents domiciled outside the state
    • Register to vote in New Hampshire
    • Avoid being claimed as a dependent by a nonresident parent
    • Remain in New Hampshire during school recesses and vacations
    • Submit the notarized Residency Statement
  3. Document your finances. Be ready to prove how you support yourself and that nobody out of state claims you as a dependent.
  4. Cut ties to your old state. Surrender the old license, cancel the old voter registration, and move your vehicle registration.
  5. Petition your university. File the reclassification application with the registrar or residency office before their deadline.

Documents to collect

  • UNH In-State Status Application
  • Notarized Residency Statement
  • New Hampshire driver's license, vehicle registration, and voter registration
  • Tax returns showing New Hampshire residence, and proof you are not claimed by an out-of-state parent
  • Proof of New Hampshire housing such as a lease, deed, or utility bills
  • Military orders, DD-214, or a VA Certificate of Eligibility for the veteran pathways

Deadlines

UNH reclassification applications are due September 1 for fall and January 1 for spring. A change approved during a semester does not take effect until the following semester.

Shortcuts and exceptions

These groups may qualify faster, or right away:

  • Any veteran who establishes residence in New Hampshire is eligible for the in-state rate immediately, with no waiting period
  • Active-duty members stationed in New Hampshire, plus spouse and dependent children
  • Spouses and dependent children of a service member reassigned out of state, if they keep living in New Hampshire
  • GI Bill Chapter 30, 31, 33, and 35 users while living in New Hampshire, regardless of length of residency
  • If you qualified through a parent's domicile and the parent moves away, in-state tuition continues for one more semester

The detail most people miss

New Hampshire treats normal student behavior as evidence against you. Policy lists regular departure from the state during recesses or vacations as evidence weighing against domicile, and an emancipation claim requires showing you did not return to your parent's home during vacations. Going home for winter and summer break can by itself defeat a residency claim.

Common mistakes

  • Starting late. The clock runs before enrollment, so a plan that begins in your first semester is already behind.
  • Keeping old-state ties. An out-of-state license, voter registration, or car tag can sink an otherwise strong case.
  • Thin documentation. Reviewers decide on paper. If you cannot prove it, it did not happen.
  • Assuming your school reads the rule loosely. Campuses vary in how strictly they apply the same statute.
