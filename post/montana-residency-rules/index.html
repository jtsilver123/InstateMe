Bottom line: Check the exceptions first

There is a structural barrier here, not just a high bar. For a typical dependent undergraduate the standard route is effectively closed, so the exceptions further down are the realistic path. Board of Regents Policy 940.1(D)(5) presumes anyone enrolled in more than half a full-time credit load is in Montana primarily for education, and the University of Montana will not let the 12-month clock start at all if you take more than 7 credits per semester. Add a bar on absences over 30 days and a full financial-independence test, and a typical enrolled out-of-state undergraduate effectively cannot convert. The controlling rule is Montana Board of Regents Policy 940.1, Residency Policy.

Who can qualify

If you are independent

Required for students not claiming through a parent. You must document that you receive less than half of your income and support from anyone else, that you pay the majority of your own expenses including cost of attendance and room and board, and that no one claims you as a dependent for the period claimed.

If your parents claim you

A dependent student qualifies only if a parent or legal guardian is themselves domiciled in Montana, and the parent must file a Montana resident tax return for the period claimed.

How to establish residency

  1. Live in Montana for 12 continuous months. Keep one fixed address, and keep proof of it for the whole period.
  2. Show intent to stay. These are the acts this state looks for:
    • Register any vehicle you own or operate in Montana
    • Obtain a Montana driver's license, or a Montana ID card if you do not drive
    • Register to vote in Montana
    • Sever ties to your former state by surrendering the old license and canceling the old voter registration
    • File a Montana resident income tax return for the period you are claiming
  3. Document your finances. Be ready to prove how you support yourself and that nobody out of state claims you as a dependent.
  4. Cut ties to your old state. Surrender the old license, cancel the old voter registration, and move your vehicle registration.
  5. Petition your university. File the reclassification application with the registrar or residency office before their deadline.

Documents to collect

  • Residency Questionnaire
  • Montana driver's license or state ID
  • Montana vehicle registration
  • Montana voter registration confirmation
  • Proof you severed ties to your former state
  • Montana resident income tax return for the period claimed
  • Notarized Tax Exemption Affidavit from a parent or guardian, required if you are under 24
  • Financial independence records such as pay stubs, bank records, and a lease

Deadlines

At the University of Montana, petitions are accepted no earlier than the 11th month of the 12-month waiting period and no later than the fifth instructional day of the semester. Appeals go to the Commissioner within 14 days.

Shortcuts and exceptions

These groups may qualify faster, or right away:

  • Montana National Guard members in good standing, plus spouses and dependent children
  • Service members stationed in Montana, plus spouses and dependent children
  • Veterans and covered individuals under federal law
  • People with full-time Montana employment of 30 or more hours a week who file a Montana resident tax return, plus spouse and dependent children
  • Montana University System employees working at least three-quarter time, plus spouse and dependent children
  • Montana high school graduates who attended their entire senior year and enroll within four fall terms of graduating
  • Note: receiving a WUE award actually creates a presumption against residency eligibility

The detail most people miss

The residency clock does not run while you are a real student. Policy 940.1(D)(5) presumes anyone registered for more than half a full-time credit load, including online credits from any school, is in Montana primarily for education, and the registrar states that taking more than 7 credits a semester prevents the waiting period from beginning at all. Being away from Montana more than 30 days during the qualifying year also triggers a presumption against you, so even a normal summer trip home can reset your case.

Common mistakes

  • Starting late. The clock runs before enrollment, so a plan that begins in your first semester is already behind.
  • Keeping old-state ties. An out-of-state license, voter registration, or car tag can sink an otherwise strong case.
  • Thin documentation. Reviewers decide on paper. If you cannot prove it, it did not happen.
  • Assuming your school reads the rule loosely. Campuses vary in how strictly they apply the same statute.
