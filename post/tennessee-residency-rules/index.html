Bottom line: Check the exceptions first

There is a structural barrier here, not just a high bar. For a typical dependent undergraduate the standard route is effectively closed, so the exceptions further down are the realistic path. Rule 1720-01-01-.05 presumes that an emancipated person does not acquire Tennessee domicile at all while enrolled at any higher-education institution in the state, rebuttable only by clear and convincing evidence. UT Knoxville states that out-of-state students do not acquire domicile while attending 'regardless of the length of time a student is enrolled,' so waiting does not help. The controlling rule is Tenn. Comp. R. & Regs. ch. 1720-01-01 (Rules of the University of Tennessee, All Campuses), adopted under Tenn. Code Ann. § 49-9-105.

Who can qualify

If you are independent

Independent status requires being age 24+, married, having children, or being a veteran/active duty. Critically, a student 'who is eligible to be claimed under the federal income tax code shall be considered as a dependent student regardless of whether the student is claimed or not.' Parent-financed, guaranteed or co-signed loans, gifts from parents, property bought by parents, 529 plans, trust funds and college savings accounts do not count as independent support. Even a fully independent student must still separately prove the move to Tennessee was not for educational purposes.

If your parents claim you

A dependent student takes the domicile of the parents, and UT Knoxville requires BOTH parents to be primarily and currently domiciled in Tennessee unless the parents are divorced, legally separated, or never married.

How to establish residency

  1. Establish a permanent home in Tennessee. Keep one fixed address, and keep proof of it for the whole period.
  2. Show intent to stay. These are the acts this state looks for:
    • driver's license
    • vehicle registration
    • voter registration
    • employment in Tennessee
    • relinquishment of prior-state domicile
  3. Document your finances. Be ready to prove how you support yourself and that nobody out of state claims you as a dependent.
  4. Cut ties to your old state. Surrender the old license, cancel the old voter registration, and move your vehicle registration.
  5. Petition your university. File the reclassification application with the registrar or residency office before their deadline.

Documents to collect

  • federal income tax returns (student and parents)
  • proof of both parents' primary Tennessee domicile
  • evidence prior-state domicile was relinquished
  • proof of lawful presence (EVEA) documentation
  • military orders / DD-214 / Home of Record documentation
  • VA Certificate of Eligibility
  • permanent resident card or qualifying visa for non-citizens

Deadlines

Last day to final register/add/change grading options for a full-session course, usually about the 7th day of the semester. UTK states no exceptions and no retroactive classification.

Shortcuts and exceptions

These groups may qualify faster, or right away:

  • active duty military stationed in Tennessee, plus spouse and dependent children
  • Fort Campbell (KY) servicemembers' children
  • veterans under the Tennessee Vets Act and 38 U.S.C. 3679 covered individuals
  • part-time students employed full-time in Tennessee (multiple employers may be combined)
  • spouse of a student already classified in-state
  • parents move out of state after in-state classification (continuous enrollment required)
  • Dependent Children Scholarship Act (fallen/disabled law enforcement, firefighters, EMTs)
  • Kentucky reciprocity for Fulton, Hickman and Graves County residents (UT Martin only)
  • limited honors/Chancellor's Scholarship consideration at UTK

The detail most people miss

Marital status of the parents flips the outcome. Under Rule 1720-01-01-.03(3), a dependent student of divorced parents is classified in-state when just ONE parent is domiciled in Tennessee, 'regardless of custodial status', but UTK states that if the parents are still married and only one lives in Tennessee, the student does not qualify at all. Roughly a $20,000/year swing turning on whether the parents are divorced.

Common mistakes

  • Starting late. The clock runs before enrollment, so a plan that begins in your first semester is already behind.
  • Keeping old-state ties. An out-of-state license, voter registration, or car tag can sink an otherwise strong case.
  • Thin documentation. Reviewers decide on paper. If you cannot prove it, it did not happen.
  • Assuming your school reads the rule loosely. Campuses vary in how strictly they apply the same statute.
