How to Get In-State Tuition in South Dakota

InstateMe research·Jul 10, 2026

Difficulty
Hard
Time required
12 monthsSouth Dakota Board of Regents Policy 3.1.1, section F.1.1, classifies a person as a resident if the person has continuously resided in South Dakota for at least twelve consecutive months prior to the first scheduled day of classes of the semester in which the individual registers, AND has not attended another post-secondary institution full time. Section F.1.2 adds that physical presence in South Dakota for the predominant purpose of attending postsecondary education does not count in determining the twelve month period, although part-time attendance or distance courses do not automatically disqualify the applicant. Section I allows nonresidents to petition for reclassification after twelve months of continuous presence.

Bottom line: Check the exceptions first

There is a structural barrier here, not just a high bar. For a typical dependent undergraduate the standard route is effectively closed, so the exceptions further down are the realistic path. The qualifying year structurally cannot accrue while the applicant is a full-time student. Policy 3.1.1 F.1.1 conditions the 12 month pathway on the person not having attended another post-secondary institution full time, and F.1.2 excludes from the count any presence whose predominant purpose is postsecondary education. Policy also provides that a student initially classified as a nonresident retains that status unless and until deemed eligible in a later semester, that the burden is clear and convincing evidence, and that the residence of an unemancipated person under twenty-four years of age follows the parents. Only part-time enrollment, marriage to a resident, a veteran or active duty status, or a parental relocation realistically breaks the loop. The controlling rule is South Dakota Board of Regents Policy 3.1.1, Resident and Nonresident Classification of Students, adopted under SDCL chapters 13-49 through 13-53; contested classification review under SDCL ch. 1-26.

Who can qualify

If you are independent

There is no numeric self-support test, but Policy 3.1.1 F.2.1 provides that the residence of an unemancipated person under twenty-four years of age follows that of the parents or legal guardian. The institution may require proof of emancipation, adoption, or guardianship. In practice a traditional-age dependent undergraduate cannot establish independent South Dakota residency without proving emancipation, marriage to a resident, or a parental move.

If your parents claim you

Yes, and it is the strongest route. Under F.2.2, unemancipated students whose parents established South Dakota residence for reasons not predominantly related to qualifying their children for reduced tuition may be classified as residents even though the family has not been in the state the full twelve months. Under F.2.3 a student stays a resident if parents leave the state after twelve months of residency, effective only from the following semester.

How to establish residency

  1. Live in South Dakota for 12 continuous months. Keep one fixed address, and keep proof of it for the whole period.
  2. Show intent to stay. These are the acts this state looks for:
    • Establish a permanent home in South Dakota to which you return in times of repose, not merely a school-year address
    • Obtain a South Dakota driver's license and register your vehicle in South Dakota
    • Register to vote in South Dakota, although the policy states voting and paying taxes alone do not establish residence
    • Pay South Dakota taxes, including property taxes, and file loan applications and federal returns showing a South Dakota residence
    • Show South Dakota-sourced income and, if possible, own property in the state
    • Join civic and community organizations in South Dakota
    • Enroll part time or in distance courses rather than full time while the twelve months run
  3. Document your finances. Be ready to prove how you support yourself and that nobody out of state claims you as a dependent.
  4. Cut ties to your old state. Surrender the old license, cancel the old voter registration, and move your vehicle registration.
  5. Petition your university. File the reclassification application with the registrar or residency office before their deadline.

Documents to collect

  • Completed Application for Resident Status filed with the campus registrar or admissions office
  • South Dakota driver's license and vehicle registration
  • Lease, mortgage, or property ownership records covering the twelve month period
  • Federal and state tax returns and loan applications showing a South Dakota residence
  • Proof of emancipation, adoption, or guardianship if under twenty-four
  • Marriage certificate for the spousal route, DD-214 or orders for the military route, high school transcript for the graduate route

Deadlines

Institutions set their own internal deadlines for the residency classification process. A student initially classified as nonresident who wants to appeal must file the Residency Application Appeal Form with supporting documentation within 15 calendar days of being notified. A granted reclassification takes effect only with the first semester or session after the petition is approved, and students who fail to request resident status before a given semester or to appeal on time are deemed to have waived any claim to the reduced rate for that semester.

Shortcuts and exceptions

These groups may qualify faster, or right away:

  • Veterans who have established permanent South Dakota residence are granted resident status and are not subject to the 12 month requirement
  • Active duty armed forces personnel stationed in South Dakota, and their spouses
  • Graduates of a South Dakota high school, and anyone who was a South Dakota resident at high school graduation living with a resident parent or guardian
  • Marriage to a South Dakota resident triggers reclassification upon timely proof of marriage, with no waiting period
  • Absence from South Dakota to pursue postsecondary education does not strip a prior resident of resident status
  • A person once properly classified as a resident stays a resident while continuously enrolled in the regental system until the degree is earned
  • International students on visas that permit establishing domicile may qualify the same way citizens do
  • Catch-all in F.1.3.3 lets institutions classify students as residents in unusual circumstances within the general scope of the policy

The detail most people miss

For most families the residency fight is unnecessary. The South Dakota Advantage program gives the resident tuition rate to new students from eleven states: Colorado, Illinois, Iowa, Kansas, Minnesota, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, North Dakota, Wisconsin, and Wyoming. Regental campuses also offer a Child of Alumni rate for nonresident children of graduates. The short pathway South Dakota is known for is this pricing program, not the domicile rule, which is one of the strictest in the region. One door the policy does leave open: Section F.1.2 says presence for the predominant purpose of attending college does not count toward the twelve months, but it then states that attending part time or taking distance courses does not automatically exclude you from the requirement. So a part-time or online enrollment pattern can let the clock run where full-time enrollment cannot.

Common mistakes

  • Starting late. The clock runs before enrollment, so a plan that begins in your first semester is already behind.
  • Keeping old-state ties. An out-of-state license, voter registration, or car tag can sink an otherwise strong case.
  • Thin documentation. Reviewers decide on paper. If you cannot prove it, it did not happen.
  • Assuming your school reads the rule loosely. Campuses vary in how strictly they apply the same statute.
